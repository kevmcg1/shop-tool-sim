Guide

Hey — here's the short version

A milling machine carves shapes out of a solid block of metal. It doesn't think; it just follows a list of instructions someone wrote for it. This page pretends to be that machine so you can watch the list happen before anyone runs it for real.

You can't break anything here. Poke at it.

Where things are

  • Big dark area in the middle — that's the machine.
  • Strip down the left — that's where you change stuff. Each gray bar is a drawer; click one to open it.
  • Box along the bottom — the instructions themselves. Just text.

Do this first

  1. Click Program on the left.
  2. Click any card under it — Drill is a good one.
  3. Hit the big yellow ▶ under the view.

The tool comes down and starts cutting. That's the whole idea.

Getting around

  • Left-click and drag — slide everything around.
  • Right-click and drag — spin around the part.
  • Shift + right-click and drag — spin around whatever is under the cursor instead.
  • Scroll wheel — closer or farther away.
  • Lost? Fit view — the ⛶ button, bottom left. It always works.

Moving the view never changes the part. You're just walking around it.

Those little buttons, bottom left

They turn things on and off. Lit means you can see it. Hover one and it tells you what it is — and the thing it controls answers back in green so you know you've got the right one.

Hiding something doesn't delete it. The metal still gets cut.

Two modes

There's a switch on the message near the bottom of the view.

  • Sim — for watching. Play, pause, scrub.
  • Model — for arranging. Click a thing to grab it, then drag the arrows.

If clicking the metal does nothing, you're in Sim. Flip the switch.

What the colors mean

The thin lines are where the tool goes. Blue is slow and careful, red is fast, gray is the tool moving through air without cutting.

When it goes sideways

  • Can't see anything? Fit view — the ⛶ button in the bottom-left stack.
  • Tool waving around in space? The block is parked wrong. Switch to Model and hit Auto align to vise and parallels up top.
  • Want that change back? Undo and Redo sit near the bottom of the view in Model mode.
  • Really made a mess? Settings ▸ Easy ▸ Hold to reset — hold the left mouse button on it for three seconds.
💡 Everything you do is saved on your own computer, so it's still here tomorrow. Settings ▸ Wipe all clears it if you want a clean slate.

What it understands

  • Motion — G00 G01 G02 G03, arcs by I/J/K or R, helical when there's a Z on the arc line.
  • Modes — G90/G91, G20/G21, G17, G54–G59, G43.
  • Canned cycles — G81, G82, G83, G84, with G98/G99 and G80.
  • Haas pockets — G12/G13: I first-cut radius, K finish radius, Q stepover.
  • Engraving — G47, I angle, J height, text in parens.
  • Rotary A B C; stops and coolant M00 M01 M03/4/5 M07/8/9 M06 M30.

Heads up: cutter comp isn't applied. G41/G42 parse and the D word lands in Tool Offsets, but the line you see is the programmed path, not the compensated centerline.

Axes and zero

Z up, X right, Y away from you. Every setup lands with work zero on the near-left corner of the top face, so X and Y run positive into the material and Z0 is the top. The vise goes in turned a quarter — jaws closing across Y, fixed jaw at the back. Before line one runs, the cutter sits on the origin.

The drawers

  • Stock — shape, material and size, all live. Change one and the blank re-skins itself a cell at a time and keeps the machining you've already done.
  • Fixture — vise, parallels, table, each with its own show/hide. Auto align parallels re-cuts the pair to the jaws and banks one against each face.
  • Tool Table — every T word found. You supply diameters; arrows step 0.025", typing applies live.
  • Tool Offsets — same deal for D words. Rows appear and vanish with the program.
  • Tool Models — real cutter geometry instead of a cylinder. Load a sample, then call it by its T number.
  • Library — parts, stock, clamps, whole scenarios. Click a row to drop it in; the panel tracks what's loaded.
  • Sim Debug — clock, current move, editor line, cutter tip.

How the cutting works

The billet isn't a mesh or a voxel grid — it's an equation. Stock is one signed distance function, every pass adds another, and the part is the first minus the union of the rest, solved per pixel on the GPU:

part(p) = max( stock(p), −cuts(p) )

So a pocket wall is exact at any zoom and a 0.001" skim leaves a 0.001" step. Scrubbing back is a truncation — drop the cuts made after that moment and the model is right again. No snapshots, no replay. Needs WebGL2. On High graphics, machined faces come up with a mirror finish.

Playback

  • Single Block pauses after every line.
  • Speed presets are a real multiplier, so Feed and RPM scale with them and get tagged @2×.
  • The two icons above the presets swap the path between feed-rate color and one color per kind of move.
  • Follow Tool pins the camera to the tip.

Model mode

Click a solid to select it. Position, Rotate and Scale are the three buttons beside the show/hide strip — arrows, rings, boxes. Undo and Redo sit above the mode banner and slide the part back rather than snapping it. Select the billet and a bar appears up top with auto-align and left / center / right along the jaws.

Hiding or showing anything disintegrates it corner to corner.

Saving

Program, tool diameters, offsets, tool models and your settings are kept in this browser on this computer. Nothing is uploaded and there's no account. Settings ▸ Wipe all clears the lot.
